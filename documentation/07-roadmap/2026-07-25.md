## Purpose

This document defines the next major gameplay tranche after the July 2026 systems expansion. It is intentionally detailed enough to be decomposed into focused implementation milestones and agent issues without requiring agents to reconstruct product intent from chat history.

The roadmap covers four connected goals:

1. Establish in-game dialogue and story progression through the end of the Swamps, culminating in recovery of the Wrong Machine.
2. Reconcile the current random splice-variant implementation with a player-owned kin reconstruction and unlock loop, then rebalance kin mechanics.
3. Turn the new `hazard`, `shrine`, and `chaos` node types into repeatable content systems with meaningful authored catalogs.
4. Introduce a persistent item foundation for lineage materials and boss catalysts, with healing and energy consumables as a stretch milestone.

This document is directional planning. Each implementation branch should still be scoped through a focused entry in `agent/ISSUES.md` and `agent/MILESTONES.md` with branch-specific acceptance criteria.

## Executive Direction

The recommended delivery sequence is:

1. **Opening Arc Completion** — add Mountains and Swamps story beats and make the Wrong Machine a backend-owned feature unlock.
2. **Progression Item Foundation** — expose lineage materials and boss catalysts as persistent account inventory.
3. **Kin Reconstruction and Balance** — replace unrestricted global variant rolling with player-unlocked kin pools and targeted reconstruction.
4. **Encounter Content Framework** — define reusable effect primitives and authoring contracts for hazards, shrines, and chaos reels.
5. **Encounter Content Pack** — seed at least ten hazards, ten shrines, and ten entries for each of the three chaos reels.
6. **General Inventory and Consumables** — generalize the item catalog and add between-encounter healing plus player energy recovery items.

The progression item foundation is not treated as stretch work. The established story loop requires players to collect lineage materials before and after recovering the Wrong Machine. Healing and energy consumables remain the stretch portion.

## Current Implemented Baseline

The implementation already provides most of the technical foundations needed for this roadmap:

- Dialogue nodes are first-class run-map nodes.
- Dialogue scripts support authored speakers, portraits, choices, one-time discovery, and feature-sensitive variants.
- Mystic Cave contains The Whim introduction and a repeat reminder to return after finding the Wrong Machine.
- The Farm contains Mudking and Tooth Collector story beats.
- The Mountains contain The Archivist's first-contact scene.
- The Swamps are unlocked after Mountains completion but do not yet contain the planned Wrong Machine story arc.
- Persistent feature and dialogue unlock state is stored through `user_unlocks`.
- Raw Chaos is a persistent player currency and dice can be salvaged into it.
- Splice variants are persisted on owned units and affect combat stats.
- Variant assignment currently rolls globally from every enabled positive-weight splice entry.
- `region_items` and `user_region_items` already support persistent region-linked item quantities.
- Existing seeded region items include boss-item concepts such as Roc Egg and Gator Head.
- Run maps recognize `hazard`, `shrine`, and `chaos` node types.
- Shrine results and chaos reel results are persisted and backend-authoritative.
- Chaos encounters support one persisted reroll, finalization, reward application, and downstream progression.
- The developer debug panel can inspect allowlisted seeded catalogs as decoded JSON.

## Product Decisions Established by This Roadmap

Unless a focused design issue explicitly changes them, the following decisions should be treated as the target direction.

### Story and Wrong Machine

- The Wrong Machine is recovered at the end of the Swamps chapter after defeating the Bog Tyrant or equivalent frog ruler.
- The Wrong Machine is a proper name, not a statement that the machine is broken.
- The backend owns the feature unlock; dialogue presents and explains the unlock but does not independently grant it.
- The first return to Mystic Cave after recovery introduces lineage reconstruction.
- The first reconstruction is guaranteed and immediately grants a usable kin goblin.

### Kin and Lineages

- “Kin,” “lineage,” and the current internal `splice_variant` concept refer to the same broad player-facing system unless later separated intentionally.
- Player-facing language should prefer **kin** or **lineage**. Existing storage and service names may continue using `splice_variant` until a dedicated terminology migration is justified.
- Kin are account-level unlocks that affect future recruitment and reward pools.
- Players may also perform a more expensive targeted reconstruction through the Wrong Machine.
- Existing units retain their kin through promotion.
- Basic Goblins remain viable and should never become an objectively obsolete failure state.
- Kin identity should remain smaller than class identity and promotion identity.

### Items

- Lineage materials and boss catalysts are core progression inventory.
- Required campaign unlocks must not depend on unprotected rare random drops.
- First-clear guarantees, deterministic progress, or pity rules must protect required items.
- Consumables are used outside automated combat rather than adding mid-combat player input.

### Encounter Content

- Hazards and shrines should be authored combinations of a small effect vocabulary, not thirty bespoke backend subsystems.
- Chaos encounters should use three combinatorial catalogs: enemy family, encounter shape, and rule/reward.
- Region eligibility and weighting must be authored data rather than hidden hard-coded special cases where practical.
- Optional kin interactions should improve or alter encounters without making a particular kin mandatory for the primary route.

## Terminology

| Term | Meaning in this roadmap |
| --- | --- |
| Kin | Player-facing inherited goblin form, currently stored as a splice variant. |
| Lineage | Account-level reconstruction unlock associated with a kin. |
| Lineage material | Stackable item recovered from a creature family and spent in the Wrong Machine. |
| Boss catalyst | Rare or guaranteed boss-linked item used for major reconstructions or feature unlocks. |
| Wrong Machine | Recovered Swamp feature that combines Raw Chaos and materials to reconstruct lineages. |
| Hazard | Non-combat run obstacle that creates attrition, penalties, or resource pressure. |
| Shrine | Non-combat run opportunity that grants a benefit, bargain, cleanse, or controlled risk. |
| Chaos encounter | Persisted three-reel combination of enemy family, encounter shape, and rule/reward. |

# Milestone 1: Opening Arc Completion

## Goal

Complete the implemented narrative arc from Mystic Cave through the Swamps and make recovery of the Wrong Machine a real account progression event.

## Player Experience

A new player should understand the following sequence without consulting external documentation:

1. The Whim manifests the player and sends the new goblins toward The Farm.
2. Defeating the Mudking frees the Tooth Collector and establishes the teeth economy.
3. In the Mountains, The Archivist notices the return of goblinkind.
4. Kobolds recognize evidence of the Wrong Machine and identify the Swamps as its current location.
5. Frog rulers treat the machine as dangerous contraband or a trophy that must not return to goblin control.
6. The Bog Tyrant refuses to surrender it.
7. The player defeats the Bog Tyrant and recovers the Wrong Machine.
8. The Whim explains that the machine can reconstruct forms stripped from goblinkind by imposed order.
9. The player performs a guaranteed first kin reconstruction.

## Required Story Beats

### Mountains: Locate the Machine

Add at least two new Mountains dialogue beats.

#### Mountains investigation dialogue

Recommended placement: optional midpoint or before boss.

Purpose:

- Establish that kobolds are competent tinkerers rather than disposable comic inventors.
- Let kobolds recognize chaos-built components, impossible mechanisms, old goblin markings, or descriptions of the machine.
- Clarify that they cannot safely reproduce the Wrong Machine.
- Point toward the Swamps without fully explaining the machine's purpose.

Possible speaker directions:

- Kobold Sharpshooter guarding a recovered component.
- Kobold Warchief or chief engineer.
- Captured or nervous kobold mechanic.

#### Mountains completion dialogue

Recommended placement: before exit, one-time.

Purpose:

- Confirm that the machine was traded, stolen, transported, or claimed by the frogmen.
- Establish the Bog Tyrant as the current holder.
- Give the player a direct narrative reason to enter the Swamps beyond regional progression.

### Swamps: Reclaim the Machine

Add at least four new Swamps dialogue beats.

#### Swamps opening dialogue

Recommended placement: start, one-time.

Purpose:

- Establish that the region operates under the Library's broader order while retaining its own local culture.
- Present the Wrong Machine as forbidden contraband, a trophy of authority, or a dangerous relic.
- Make clear that local frogmen have reasons to fear its return beyond simply obeying the Archivist.

#### Swamps investigation dialogue

Recommended placement: optional midpoint branch, one-time or repeatable variant.

Purpose:

- Reveal that the machine constructs from chaos.
- Reinforce that its strange behavior is intentional rather than malfunction.
- Foreshadow lineage materials already collected in earlier regions.

#### Swamps boss confrontation

Recommended placement: before boss, repeatable with first-visit and repeat variants.

Purpose:

- Let the Bog Tyrant articulate why the machine will not be surrendered.
- Give the player dialogue choices that establish goblin voice without allowing dialogue to bypass the boss fight.
- Preserve the Bog Tyrant as a potential recurring regional ruler after defeat.

#### Swamps machine recovery

Recommended placement: after boss and before exit, one-time.

Purpose:

- Present the recovered Wrong Machine.
- Grant or confirm the `wrong_machine` feature unlock.
- Tell the player to return to Mystic Cave or directly unlock the relevant home navigation.
- Avoid explaining every reconstruction rule in the same scene.

### Mystic Cave: Reconstruction Tutorial

Add a feature-gated, one-time dialogue that appears after the Wrong Machine is unlocked.

Purpose:

- Replace the existing repeat reminder with a post-recovery response.
- Explain that order did not only kill goblins; it stabilized or separated their possible forms.
- Introduce Raw Chaos and lineage materials as the machine's inputs.
- Direct the player into the guaranteed first reconstruction.

## State Ownership

The recommended account state is:

- `feature / wrong_machine`: granted by backend progression when the qualifying Swamps completion or boss claim succeeds.
- `dialogue / <dialogue-id>`: records one-time story discovery as already implemented.
- `lineage / <lineage-slug>`: future account-level reconstructed lineage unlock.
- Existing Raw Chaos currency remains in player state.
- Existing or expanded item ownership stores lineage materials and catalysts.

The feature unlock must be idempotent. Replaying, retrying, refreshing, or claiming the same qualifying reward must not grant duplicate one-time rewards.

## Recommended Unlock Trigger

Preferred trigger:

- First successful claim of the Swamps boss result or first successful Swamps run completion.

The exact trigger should follow whichever lifecycle point already owns durable feature grants and is protected by claim idempotency. The dialogue node should reflect the durable state rather than race it.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Mountains contains an authored route from Archivist discovery to locating the Wrong Machine in the Swamps.
- Swamps contains opening, investigation, boss, and recovery dialogue.
- First qualifying Swamps completion grants the Wrong Machine feature exactly once.
- The Wrong Machine feature is visible in the profile or feature-unlock payload.
- A player with the feature sees post-recovery Mystic Cave dialogue rather than the old reminder.
- A player without the feature cannot access reconstruction actions.
- Story nodes remain removable or replaceable after one-time discovery without breaking graph connectivity.
- Repeat Swamps runs use appropriate repeat dialogue rather than replaying all first-clear exposition.
- Tests cover fresh player, returning player, already-unlocked player, replay, and retry behavior.

## Deliberately Deferred

- Full animated Wrong Machine interaction.
- Dice fabrication.
- Bulk salvage.
- Complex branching story outcomes.
- Dialogue choices that permanently change campaign state.
- Multiple possible Swamps rulers.

# Milestone 2: Progression Item Foundation

## Goal

Expose a durable item inventory sufficient to support lineage materials, boss catalysts, hidden-route rewards, and Wrong Machine costs before implementing general consumables.

## Existing Foundation to Reuse

The repository already has:

- `region_items` definitions linked to regions.
- `user_region_items` ownership with quantity.
- Profile support for region items.
- Seeded boss-item concepts including Roc Egg and Gator Head.

This is sufficient for a minimal progression-material wallet, but the current definition is too narrow for a general inventory because it only carries region, slug, and name.

## Recommended Minimal Item Categories

The first implementation should support at least:

- `lineage_material`
- `boss_catalyst`
- `machine_catalyst`
- `unlock_key`

These categories may be added to the existing region-item catalog or introduced through a generic item catalog. The choice should follow the seed-ownership architecture review, but the player contract should not depend on the physical table name.

## Required Definition Fields

Each progression item should eventually expose:

- stable slug
- display name
- description
- category
- source region or source family
- rarity or progression tier
- stackability
- optional icon/art reference
- whether it is visible before first discovery
- whether it may be spent
- whether it is required for primary progression
- optional codex or lore reference

## Initial Material Families

At minimum, seed materials for the first three enemy families:

| Enemy family | Common lineage material | Boss catalyst direction | Tutorial use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pigs / Farm | Pig Ear, Mud-Tough Hide, or Tusk Shard | Mudking Crown Fragment or Royal Tusk | Recommended first reconstruction. |
| Kobolds / Mountains | Scale Scrap, Tinker Claw, or Shed Crest | Warchief Gear-Core or Roc Egg if retained | Unlock Lizard/Kobold kin. |
| Frogmen / Swamps | Bog Skin, Resonant Throat Sac, or Webbed Claw | Gator Head or Bog Tyrant Totem | Unlock Frog kin or Wrong Machine upgrade. |

Names remain content-direction choices. The implementation should not bind behavior to English display names.

## Acquisition Model

### Common materials

Common lineage materials should come from repeated play against the relevant family.

Recommended sources:

- combat and boss reward tables
- optional hazard outcomes
- hidden kin routes
- bounty rewards
- chaos reward reel outcomes

### Boss catalysts

Boss catalysts should be:

- guaranteed on first qualifying boss completion when required for campaign progression
- rare but repeatable afterward
- protected by idempotent claim logic
- clearly identified as important rather than visually indistinguishable vendor trash

### Materials collected before the Wrong Machine

Players may collect Farm and Mountains materials before recovering the machine. Before unlock:

- quantities should persist
- item descriptions may hint at an unknown use
- the inventory may show them as curios, trophies, or unidentified machine materials
- players should not be allowed to spend them

After recovery:

- the same items become visible as lineage ingredients
- any tutorial requirements should count already-owned quantities

## Progression Protection Rules

- Primary campaign progression must not require an unprotected random drop.
- A required catalyst should be a first-clear guarantee or deterministic milestone reward.
- Common materials may use random quantities, but a player should make visible progress every few eligible encounters.
- Reward claims must be idempotent.
- Item quantities must never become negative.
- Spending must be backend-authoritative and transactional with Raw Chaos spending and lineage unlock grants.

## Initial Player-Facing Surface

The first item UI does not need to be a fully generalized inventory screen. A minimal progression-material surface may live inside the Wrong Machine or an existing inventory page and should show:

- item icon or placeholder
- name
- owned quantity
- source region/family
- known use or undiscovered state
- required amount for a selected reconstruction

## Acceptance Criteria

- Players can earn, persist, inspect, and spend lineage materials.
- Materials earned before the Wrong Machine remain available afterward.
- At least one common material and one boss catalyst exist for Farm, Mountains, and Swamps.
- Required first-reconstruction materials are protected from random failure.
- Reward preview and claim surfaces display newly granted progression items.
- Profile and debug tooling expose quantities.
- Spending Raw Chaos and materials for reconstruction is atomic and idempotent.
- Tests cover duplicate claims, insufficient quantity, concurrent spend attempts, and already-unlocked lineages.

# Milestone 3: Kin Reconstruction and Balance

## Goal

Turn splice variants from a global random grant modifier into a coherent account progression system connected to enemy families, the Wrong Machine, and long-term build choice.

## Current Design Conflict

The current variant service rolls from all enabled variants with positive weights. This means Rat, Toad, and Bat variants may appear before the player has discovered or reconstructed those lineages.

That prototype behavior conflicts with the intended progression loop:

1. Fight a creature family.
2. Recover materials.
3. Recover the Wrong Machine.
4. Spend materials and Raw Chaos.
5. Unlock the lineage.
6. Recruit or reconstruct goblins of that kin.
7. Use combat and traversal traits to access new strategies and content.

## Recommended Ownership Model

### Account-level lineage unlocks

Store permanent player lineage unlocks separately from owned unit instances.

Conceptual state:

- `lineage / basic_goblin`: implicitly available to all players.
- `lineage / pig_kin`: granted by reconstruction.
- `lineage / lizard_kin`: granted by reconstruction.
- `lineage / frog_kin`: granted by reconstruction.
- Later lineages follow the same contract.

### Recruitment and reward pools

When granting a new goblin:

- only Basic plus the player's unlocked lineages are eligible
- each eligible lineage may have an authored recruitment weight
- Basic should retain a meaningful weight
- content sources may further filter or bias the eligible pool
- deterministic rewards may explicitly grant a selected lineage

### Targeted reconstruction

The Wrong Machine should offer a direct option that creates or transforms a goblin into a chosen unlocked kin at a higher cost than ordinary random recruitment.

Initial recommendation:

- reconstruction creates a new Tier I unit of an eligible starter family
- transformation of existing units is deferred unless product design explicitly wants it
- the first tutorial reconstruction is free or discounted and guarantees the selected kin

### Recommended first lineage

Use **Pig Kin** as the tutorial reconstruction because:

- players have encountered pigs for the longest time
- Farm materials may already be in inventory
- it demonstrates retroactive value in earlier-region drops
- it creates an immediate reason to revisit the Farm
- it avoids making the Swamp's own Frog Kin feel automatically selected only because it is newest

Frog Kin remains a valid alternative if art and content availability make it substantially easier.

## Kin Mechanical Structure

Each lineage should be authored through three conceptually separate layers.

### Combat tilt

A small stat package that changes tendencies without creating a strict upgrade.

Available current stats:

- Attack
- Defense
- Max HP
- Precision
- Resolve

### Kin passive

A narrow readable mechanic such as:

- status resistance
- formation behavior
- first-hit behavior
- conditional damage
- healing interaction
- hazard protection

Passives should use a shared vocabulary where possible rather than requiring a unique combat subsystem for every lineage.

### Traversal trait

An exploration capability used to alter optional routes, hazards, rewards, shortcuts, or discoveries.

Traversal value should not necessarily consume the full combat power budget because it does not directly increase average battle output. It should, however, be considered in overall lineage desirability.

## Balance Principles

- Basic Goblins remain viable at every stage.
- General-purpose kin should remain within approximately 5% of Basic performance across a mixed representative encounter set.
- A specialized kin may substantially outperform in its intended matchup if it has real disadvantages elsewhere.
- Kin bonuses should be smaller than class and promotion differences.
- No kin should combine best-in-class offense, defense, and traversal.
- Negative modifiers must create meaningful tradeoffs rather than cosmetic penalties.
- Precision, Resolve, Attack, Defense, and HP must not be valued as equal points without simulation evidence.
- Passive effects need an explicit expected-value estimate or test scenario.
- Recruitment rarity must not be used as the main justification for raw power.

## Required Simulation Review

Before locking final numbers, run deterministic or repeated combat simulations using representative units from each major class family.

Track at least:

- win rate
- average remaining squad HP
- average rounds to resolution
- hit rate
- critical rate
- status application rate
- status resistance rate
- performance by formation position
- performance against pigs, kobolds, frogmen, and mixed future enemies

The output should compare Basic against each candidate kin and identify classes where a kin's modifier becomes disproportionately strong.

## Candidate Kin Directions

The following are starting hypotheses, not locked balance values.

| Kin | Combat identity | Candidate passive | Traversal identity | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | Flexible baseline | No penalty; optional small adaptability benefit later | No dedicated key | Must not feel like a failed roll. |
| Pig | Durable bruiser | First physical hit each battle deals reduced damage | Break weak barricades or push heavy obstacles | HP/Defense stacking may dominate guardians. |
| Lizard/Kobold | Precise tinkerer | Improved interaction with traps or first ranged action | Disarm mechanical hazards and open device routes | Precision can overperform on ranged classes. |
| Frog | Resilient control | Improved poison/slow resistance or stronger debuff duration | Cross bog water, resist spores, use flooded paths | Resolve plus status immunity may erase encounter mechanics. |
| Rat | Opportunistic scavenger | Bonus when attacking already-injured targets or improved loot find | Enter narrow passages and scavenger caches | Can become universally optimal if bonus is too broad. |
| Toad | Stubborn anchor | Reduced forced movement or stronger recovery from low HP | Resist poison/spores and cross toxic terrain | Overlaps Frog and defensive class identity. |
| Bat | Fragile precision | Reveal hidden targets or improve first strike | Reach high ledges and separated routes | Flight is extremely attractive even with weak combat stats. |
| Bug | Formation swarm | Bonus when adjacent to another Bug or support unit | Burrow through unstable tunnels | Adjacency scaling may reward monoculture squads. |
| Shadow | Evasive saboteur | Increased resistance to targeted attacks after acting | Pass narrow seals and incomplete barriers | Evasion can create frustrating variance. |
| Stone | Heavy protector | Reduced critical damage or improved guard reactions | Break cracked walls and withstand crushing hazards | Defense scaling may trivialize attrition. |

## Existing Variant Review

Current packages should be treated as temporary tuning values:

- Rat: Precision up, Resolve down.
- Toad: HP and Resolve up, Precision down.
- Bat: Attack and Precision up, HP down.

Specific concerns:

- Bat combines two offensive benefits for one relatively small HP penalty.
- Toad has more positive raw points than Rat, although raw points may not reflect actual value.
- Rat's tradeoff may be nearly invisible on units that do not rely on Resolve.
- Existing names may not match the story's enemy-family reconstruction sequence.

## Acceptance Criteria

- New-unit variant rolling filters by player-unlocked lineage state.
- Basic is always eligible unless an explicit deterministic reward overrides it.
- The Wrong Machine can reconstruct at least one lineage through a transactional backend action.
- The first reconstruction grants a usable kin unit and cannot be duplicated through retries.
- Unit promotion preserves lineage.
- Shop, reward, and unit-detail surfaces show kin identity consistently.
- The current Rat, Toad, and Bat packages are reviewed against combat simulations.
- Final balance notes document expected strengths, weaknesses, and traversal value.
- Tests cover players with zero, one, and multiple unlocked lineages.

## Deliberately Deferred

- Breeding.
- Multiple lineages on one unit.
- Inherited lineage probability from parent units.
- Free unlimited transformation of existing units.
- Lineage-specific class trees.
- Perfect deterministic control over every random recruit.

# Milestone 4: Encounter Content Framework

## Goal

Create a reusable authoring model so hazards, shrines, and chaos nodes can receive substantial content without adding bespoke application logic for every entry.

## Shared Authoring Principles

Every encounter definition should support:

- stable slug
- display title
- short description
- eligible regions
- minimum progression requirements
- weight
- repeatability or repetition limits
- effect primitive and parameters
- reward primitive and parameters
- optional kin interactions
- optional feature requirements
- player-facing result copy
- debug-readable authored JSON

Behavior-bearing slugs must have parity tests between seeded data and application handlers.

## Hazard Effect Vocabulary

Initial hazards should compose a limited set of primitives:

1. Damage one deployed unit.
2. Damage the lowest-HP deployed unit.
3. Damage multiple deployed units.
4. Apply a temporary next-battle stat penalty.
5. Apply a temporary status or environmental condition.
6. Lose teeth, Raw Chaos, or a non-critical item quantity.
7. Offer a choice between attrition and resource loss.
8. Grant a reduced or improved outcome based on an eligible kin trait.
9. Grant a reward when successfully bypassed.
10. Clear safely with no reward.

Hazards should not defeat the entire squad without a clear preview and bounded outcome. Required campaign routes should not become impossible because the player lacks an optional kin.

## Shrine Effect Vocabulary

Initial shrines should compose:

1. Heal one unit.
2. Heal all deployed units by a small amount.
3. Cleanse a temporary penalty.
4. Grant a next-battle stat buff.
5. Grant hazard protection.
6. Exchange HP for teeth, Raw Chaos, or item rewards.
7. Improve the next reward multiplier.
8. Improve one unit's mastery or XP without replacing combat progression.
9. Temporarily enhance a kin trait.
10. Offer a choice between two bounded favors.

Shrine outcomes should be backend-generated, persisted, and idempotent. The player should understand the cost before accepting a bargain.

## Chaos Reel Contract

Chaos encounters should retain three authored reel responsibilities:

1. **Enemy family** — source roster or mixed-family pressure.
2. **Encounter shape** — formation, count, elite distribution, or tactical composition.
3. **Rule and reward** — combat modifier, environmental rule, risk, and payout hook.

Ten entries in each reel produce up to 1,000 combinations before exclusions. Combinations must be validated so unsupported enemy-family and encounter-shape pairings are not generated.

## Region Population Rules

- Mountains and Swamps should use authored node weights rather than receiving identical content pools.
- Hazards should be eligible for procedural placement after the framework exists.
- Shrines should remain optional branches or pauses rather than required progression gates.
- Chaos nodes should continue to be reachable and should not replace the guaranteed boss route.
- A run should avoid presenting the same authored encounter repeatedly unless the entry explicitly allows repetition.
- Region identity should remain legible even when entries are shared.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Hazard, shrine, and chaos catalogs are visible in the seed browser.
- Application effect handlers use a bounded documented vocabulary.
- Unknown behavior slugs fail safely and visibly in development.
- Region eligibility and weights are testable.
- Hazard nodes are actually populated in eligible procedural runs.
- Unsupported chaos combinations are excluded or resolved through explicit fallback.
- Result generation and reward application remain backend-authoritative and idempotent.

# Milestone 5: Encounter Content Pack

## Goal

Seed enough content that new node types appear varied during real runs rather than feeling like technical placeholders.

The minimum pack contains:

- 10 hazards
- 10 shrines
- 10 chaos enemy-family entries
- 10 chaos encounter-shape entries
- 10 chaos rule/reward entries

The following examples are authoring direction. Exact numerical values should be tuned against run length and current attrition.

## Hazard Catalog: Initial Ten

### 1. Sucking Mud

- **Regions:** Swamps
- **Effect:** Damage or temporarily reduce Defense for the heaviest/front-most deployed unit.
- **Kin interaction:** Frog or Toad kin reduces the penalty; Bat kin bypasses it.
- **Reward:** None by default; small material chance on successful bypass.
- **Purpose:** Teach kin-based hazard mitigation without making it mandatory.

### 2. Razor Reeds

- **Regions:** Swamps
- **Effect:** Small damage to multiple deployed units.
- **Kin interaction:** Frog kin reveals a safe route; Stone kin reduces damage.
- **Reward:** Bog lineage material on the improved route.
- **Purpose:** Squad-wide attrition pressure.

### 3. Kobold Tripwire

- **Regions:** Mountains
- **Effect:** Damage one random or leading unit and apply a next-battle Precision penalty.
- **Kin interaction:** Lizard/Kobold kin disarms it and may recover a component.
- **Reward:** Tinker material when disarmed.
- **Purpose:** Mechanical-hazard identity for Mountains.

### 4. Spore Cloud

- **Regions:** Swamps, future fungal regions
- **Effect:** Apply a temporary Resolve or Precision penalty to deployed units.
- **Kin interaction:** Frog, Toad, or Bug kin gains resistance.
- **Reward:** Spore catalyst chance if safely harvested.
- **Purpose:** Introduce environmental resistance checks.

### 5. Unstable Rope Bridge

- **Regions:** Mountains
- **Effect:** Choose between squad damage and losing a small quantity of teeth while securing passage.
- **Kin interaction:** Bat kin bypasses; Stone kin stabilizes.
- **Reward:** Optional hidden cache on a successful kin route.
- **Purpose:** Explicit resource-versus-attrition choice.

### 6. Territorial Stampede

- **Regions:** Farm, Mountains
- **Effect:** Damage front-row units or reorder an upcoming encounter modifier.
- **Kin interaction:** Pig kin holds the line and reduces damage.
- **Reward:** Pig lineage material if the stampede is redirected.
- **Purpose:** Connect Farm identity to later procedural content.

### 7. Flash-Flood Channel

- **Regions:** Swamps
- **Effect:** Damage the lowest-HP unit or apply a next-battle speed/order analogue only if such vocabulary exists; otherwise use Defense/Precision penalty.
- **Kin interaction:** Frog kin crosses safely; Bat kin scouts a higher path.
- **Reward:** Hidden shrine or loot branch when bypassed.
- **Purpose:** Route-changing environmental hazard.

### 8. Crumbling Mine Shaft

- **Regions:** Mountains, future Stoneworks
- **Effect:** Damage all deployed units lightly or consume a support item to brace the path.
- **Kin interaction:** Stone kin prevents collapse; Bug kin finds a tunnel.
- **Reward:** Ore or machine catalyst.
- **Purpose:** Preview future traversal lineages.

### 9. Gator Nest

- **Regions:** Swamps
- **Effect:** High single-target damage or optional resource sacrifice to distract the nest.
- **Kin interaction:** Frog kin identifies safe water; Pig kin can absorb a reduced hit.
- **Reward:** Gator-linked boss or lineage material at bounded odds.
- **Purpose:** High-pressure optional branch hazard.

### 10. Archivist Survey Ward

- **Regions:** Mountains, Swamps
- **Effect:** Apply an Order Mark that increases the next combat's difficulty or reduces its reward unless disrupted.
- **Kin interaction:** Shadow kin bypasses; Lizard kin disables; Basic can pay teeth or accept the modifier.
- **Reward:** Raw Chaos when the ward is corrupted rather than bypassed.
- **Purpose:** Tie environmental hazards to the central antagonist.

## Shrine Catalog: Initial Ten

### 1. Shrine of Sharp Teeth

- **Effect:** Exchange a small amount of current unit HP for teeth.
- **Choice:** Conservative or greedy offering.
- **Theme:** Goblin victory, trophies, and appetite.

### 2. Shrine of Stubborn Mud

- **Effect:** Heal or grant temporary Defense/Resolve to one unit.
- **Kin interaction:** Pig or Toad kin receives an improved but bounded version.
- **Theme:** Farm and Swamp durability.

### 3. Shrine of the Open Wound

- **Effect:** Sacrifice squad HP to improve the next combat or boss reward.

# Milestone 6: General Inventory and Consumables

## Goal

Generalize the progression-item foundation into a player-facing inventory capable of storing and consuming unit-healing items and player-energy recovery items.

This is the stretch milestone. It should not block the Wrong Machine, lineage materials, or encounter content.

## Recommended Item Categories

Extend the item vocabulary to include:

- `unit_consumable`
- `player_consumable`
- optional `run_consumable`

The implementation may retain progression materials in the same generic catalog or migrate/bridge existing region-item records. Avoid maintaining two competing player inventory systems without an explicit boundary.

## Recommended Definition Fields

A generalized item definition should support:

- slug
- name
- description
- category
- rarity
- stack limit
- effect slug
- effect parameters
- valid use context
- target type
- source tags
- sell/salvage policy
- icon/art reference
- discovery and visibility rules

## Unit Healing Rules

Initial recommendation:

- Healing items are used between encounters, not during automated combat.
- The target must be a unit deployed in the active run unless an item explicitly supports roster healing.
- Healing cannot exceed max HP.
- Initial items cannot revive defeated units.
- Item consumption and HP updates are transactional.
- Refreshing or retrying cannot consume the item twice.
- Consider limiting consumable use to one item per cleared node or another bounded cadence to preserve attrition.
- Rest nodes must retain a distinct value even after consumables exist.

## Player Energy Recovery Rules

Initial recommendation:

- Energy items are used from the home, inventory, or region-selection surface.
- Initial items do not raise energy above maximum.
- Energy items remain rare enough that regeneration and region costs retain meaning.
- Suitable sources include bounties, hidden routes, optional bosses, and rare chaos rewards.
- Avoid selling unlimited energy items for teeth in the first implementation.

# Key Risks

## Kin power inflation

Adding kin passives on top of stat bonuses can make lineage choice more important than class choice. Mitigate through simulation, bounded passives, and keeping Basic competitive.

## Required-drop frustration

Rare boss items can create exciting optional goals but are inappropriate as unprotected required progression. Separate guaranteed first-clear catalysts from rare repeat drops.

## Content framework overengineering

A completely generic rule engine may cost more than the initial content warrants. Start with a small explicit primitive allowlist and add vocabulary only when multiple authored entries need it.

## Inventory duplication

Extending `region_items` and separately creating a general inventory can create two ownership systems. Decide whether to migrate, bridge, or explicitly separate progression keys from consumables before adding broad UI.

## Attrition collapse

Frequent healing items may eliminate the value of rest nodes and squad durability. Restrict context, quantity, and cadence; keep healing rewards scarce enough to preserve run decisions.

## Energy-economy bypass

Common energy items may invalidate regeneration and region costs. Keep them rare, capped, and primarily sourced from achievements, bounties, or optional difficult content.

## Story-state race conditions

Dialogue and feature unlocks can become inconsistent if separate requests own the state transition. Grant progression through one durable backend lifecycle point and let dialogue read the result.

4. Exact material names, quantities, Raw Chaos costs, and drop rates.
5. Whether boss catalysts are consumed during lineage reconstruction.
6. Whether kin traversal traits affect graph generation before a run or choices inside an already-generated run.
7. Whether consumables are limited per node, per run, or only by inventory scarcity.
8. Whether energy items restore energy directly or reduce the next run's cost.
9. Whether hazards expose exact numerical outcomes before resolution.
10. Which initial kin passives are supported by the first shared behavior vocabulary.
