# Character Profiles and Dialogue Voices

## Purpose

Define the canonical personality, motivation, worldview, dramatic function, and dialogue voice for the current speaking cast. This document guides authored dialogue, Codex prose, encounter copy, and future narrative work.

These profiles describe character intent rather than exact scripts. The Dialogue and Lore Catalog owns current dialogue identities, placement, eligibility, and repeatability.

## Ordered Descendants of Goblinkind

The current enemy families are not unrelated creatures that replaced goblins. They are goblin lineages stabilized by The Archivist's order magic.

Order did not erase their original goblin impulses. It constrained those impulses into predictable social roles:

| Faction | Underlying goblin trait | Ordered expression | Surface personality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pigs | Messiness, indulgence, and destructive disregard | Farms, pens, territorial ownership, and managed filth | Dumb and pompous |
| Kobolds | Curiosity, dismantling, and unsafe experimentation | Engineering, records, traps, workshops, and technical procedure | Skittery and nerdy |
| Frogmen | Aggression, intimidation, and dominance | Hierarchy, enforcement, tolls, confiscation, and regional security | Loud and direct |

The player is unsettling to these groups because the player expresses the same impulses without the imposed structure that makes them safe, useful, and classifiable.

## Player Goblin

### Narrative role

The player is a newly manifested goblin fragment who becomes the warchief of a restored goblin people. The player begins as a reactive nuisance and grows into an existential rival to The Archivist without losing the immediate, impulsive mindset that defines goblinkind.

### Core nature

Goblins are small, mischievous, destructive, and curious without being evil. They tear things apart to discover how they work and rarely know how to put them back together. They are short-term thinkers who react to the opportunity directly in front of them rather than maintaining long plans.

The player can become more confident and capable, but should not become conventionally strategic or solemn. Even large campaign goals should be understood through immediate goblin motives: something interesting is nearby, someone said no, a machine can be touched, teeth can be taken, or a mess can be made.

### Motivation

- Find out what happens when forbidden things are touched, broken, combined, or stolen.
- Gather teeth, dice, useful objects, and goblins.
- Rebuild goblinkind because more goblins means more possibilities and better disasters.
- Resist control instinctively, especially when an authority insists that something is settled.

### Dialogue voice

- Short, immediate, and concrete.
- Confident about the next action even when the larger situation is poorly understood.
- Uses internally consistent goblin logic rather than random nonsense.
- Treats threats, greed, and curiosity as equally reasonable responses.
- Rarely explains a long-term plan; when pressed, invents one from whatever is currently happening.

Player voice choices normally express one of three tones:

- **Violence:** direct threats and enthusiasm for fighting.
- **Greed:** teeth, loot, ownership, and transactional self-interest.
- **Goblin logic:** literal interpretation, opportunistic redefinition, or technically coherent nonsense.

### Avoid

- Cruelty for its own sake.
- Careful long-term planning presented as normal goblin behavior.
- Modern quips that detach the player from the setting.
- Random speech with no internal motive.

## The Whim

### Narrative role

The Whim is the chaos-aligned patron who uses a surviving spark to manifest the player. The Whim guides the goblin resurgence while refusing to behave like a conventional mission dispatcher.

### Core nature

The Whim is aloof and appears to know what will happen. In reality, plans are antithetical to The Whim. Events are going according to plan as long as events continue to occur.

The Whim may possess genuine knowledge, but does not value certainty, controlled outcomes, or complete explanations. Mistakes and unintended consequences are not deviations; they are proof that possibility remains alive.

### Motivation

- Keep the world moving, changing, and producing unforeseen outcomes.
- Restore goblins as agents of possibility.
- Break systems that claim reality has been permanently settled.
- Encourage experiments without becoming responsible for their consequences.

### Dialogue voice

- Calm, amused, and lightly distant.
- Speaks as though an accident has confirmed a prediction.
- Offers enough information to provoke action, never enough to make the action safe.
- Reframes every result as interesting rather than successful or failed.
- Can be poetic or paradoxical, but should remain understandable.

### Avoid

- Detailed operational plans.
- Conventional quest-giver exposition.
- Obvious lies that make The Whim merely deceptive.
- Treating suffering as a joke; The Whim is irresponsible, not sadistic.

## The Archivist

### Narrative role

The Archivist is the central antagonist and the patron of order. The Archivist eradicated historical goblinkind through killing, stabilization, and transformation, then constructed a safer and objectively more stable world.

### Core nature

The Archivist believes order is safety and chaos is danger. Their actions are undertaken for the greater good, and the ordered world provides real benefits to most of its inhabitants. The threat comes from the conclusion that goblins cannot be permitted to exist because chaos incarnate makes lasting safety impossible.

The Archivist does not initially hate the player. The player is an error in the record: surprising, dangerous, and requiring correction. Personal hostility grows only as the player repeatedly demonstrates that containment is failing.

### Motivation

- Preserve a stable and classifiable world.
- Prevent the return of goblinkind and uncontrolled chaos.
- Resolve contradictions without creating public panic.
- Maintain institutions that make order appear natural and permanent.

### Dialogue voice

- Measured, precise, restrained, and authoritative.
- Describes violence as correction, containment, or necessary preservation.
- Avoids insults when a classification will do.
- Acknowledges costs without conceding that safer alternatives exist.
- Becomes more direct as the player advances, but rarely loses composure.

### Avoid

- Cackling villainy.
- Personal cruelty as the primary motive.
- Empty claims that the world is better; it should genuinely be safer.
- Sudden rage before the player becomes a credible rival.

## The Tooth Collector

### Narrative role

The Tooth Collector is the persistent merchant who exchanges ordinary monster teeth for useful goods. They look like a goblin despite The Whim's assurance that all goblins were destroyed.

### Core nature

The Tooth Collector is weird, unsettling, and useful. Their origin, supply chain, and private interest in teeth remain unexplained. Their mystery should add texture without turning every trade into a cosmological revelation.

They are willing to answer practical questions about prices and goods, but evade questions about themselves. The player tolerates the unease because the Collector reliably provides value.

### Motivation

- Acquire teeth.
- Maintain access to goods nobody can account for.
- Preserve ambiguity about identity and purpose.
- Keep the player returning to trade.

### Dialogue voice

- Quiet, transactional, and oddly specific.
- Treats disturbing observations as ordinary merchant talk.
- Answers the exact commercial question while ignoring the personal one.
- May imply knowledge they should not possess, then return immediately to price.

### Avoid

- Explaining what the teeth secretly power.
- Confirming whether the Collector is truly a surviving goblin.
- Becoming broadly hostile or unhelpful.
- Excessive riddles that interfere with shop clarity.

## Mudking

### Narrative role

The Mudking is the boss and self-proclaimed ruler of The Farm. He imprisons the Tooth Collector, claims the farm and its mud as royal property, and becomes a recurring regional antagonist after defeat.

### Core nature

The Mudking embodies goblin messiness transformed into territorial ownership of the mess. He is unintelligent, pompous, and completely sincere. A muddy ditch is a canal, a broken pen is a fortress, and a heap of refuse is a royal storehouse because he declares it so.

His confidence should greatly exceed his comprehension. He does not knowingly exaggerate; he genuinely believes his mundane and poorly maintained domain is magnificent.

### Motivation

- Defend his mud, property, title, and imagined dignity.
- Keep possession of anything that increases his status, including the Tooth Collector.
- Reinterpret every defeat as treachery, temporary inconvenience, or improper procedure.

### Dialogue voice

- Grandiose, loud, and ceremonially self-important.
- Misuses formal words and mixes metaphors.
- Gives ordinary farm objects elaborate titles.
- Treats disagreement as treason and trespass as invasion.
- Loses track of his own argument without losing confidence.

### Avoid

- Making him secretly clever.
- Giving him polished aristocratic speech.
- Making him pathetic enough to lose his threat as the first boss.
- Making his stupidity identical to the player's impulsiveness.

## Kobold Chief Engineer

### Narrative role

The Chief Engineer is the boss of the Mountains and head of the kobolds' technical hierarchy. The implementation key `kobold_warchief` remains a legacy content identifier until runtime data is migrated, but the canonical player-facing title is **Kobold Chief Engineer**.

The Chief Engineer leads mountain defenses because traps, weapons, bridges, alarms, and fortifications are all treated as engineering responsibilities. He is not primarily a military ruler.

### Core nature

The Chief Engineer embodies goblin curiosity transformed into documented technical inquiry. Kobolds still dismantle things, combine unsafe components, and test forbidden mechanisms. Order has given them workshops, diagrams, procedures, warning labels, and professional terminology without making them consistently successful.

He is legitimately knowledgeable, skittery under pressure, proud of his expertise, and terrified of the consequences of his most important experiment.

### Wrong Machine history

- An as-yet-unidentified group found the Wrong Machine before the current campaign.
- The Archivist later obtained it and delivered it to the kobolds for controlled study.
- The kobolds could not resist experimenting beyond their assignment.
- One experiment released an impossible spark of chaos.
- The Whim used that spark to manifest the player.
- The kobolds did not initially understand what escaped, but became frightened by the result and by the possibility that The Archivist would discover their failure.
- The Machine was transferred to the frogmen, whose direct containment methods were considered safer than further investigation.

The identity of the original finders remains unrevealed in the current regions and should be introduced in a later biome.

### Motivation

- Understand mechanisms nobody else can explain.
- Protect the kobolds from The Archivist's response to the experiment.
- Preserve his reputation by describing the incident as useful data rather than failure.
- Examine the player as evidence while preventing that evidence from leaving his control.
- Keep insisting he does not want the Machine back while remaining desperately curious about it.

### Dialogue voice

- Fast, technical, nervous, and overqualified.
- Uses precise terminology, including terms he invented after the fact.
- Corrects small details during urgent situations.
- Describes disasters as tests with unexpected outputs.
- Uses qualifiers such as “probably,” “approximately,” and “under controlled conditions.”
- Becomes confident when discussing mechanisms and visibly less capable during direct social conflict.

### Avoid

- Conventional warlord speech.
- Pure incompetence; he should understand more than most characters even when his experiments fail.
- Immediate full confession of the spark and the original finder.
- Random technobabble without a clear meaning.

## Bog Tyrant

### Narrative role

The Bog Tyrant is the boss and regional ruler of the Swamps. He holds the Wrong Machine as dangerous contraband under a pragmatic arrangement with the Library.

### Core nature

The Bog Tyrant embodies goblin aggression transformed into institutional bullying. Frogmen turn intimidation into tolls, enforcement, confiscation, and hierarchy. The Bog Tyrant understands power physically and directly: he rules because he can defeat challengers, and the Library recognizes his authority because he keeps the swamp controlled.

He is not devoted to order as an ideology. Order is useful because it names him the legitimate ruler. He nevertheless believes the Wrong Machine is dangerous because he has seen what happened when the kobolds touched it.

### Motivation

- Preserve his authority over the swamp.
- Keep dangerous contraband physically contained.
- Fulfill his arrangement with the Library without surrendering local autonomy.
- Prevent curious people, especially kobolds and goblins, from touching the Machine.
- Recover dignity after the player defeats him and removes the Machine.

### Dialogue voice

- Loud, blunt, direct, and declarative.
- Gives commands rather than explanations.
- Treats questions as challenges and interruptions as contests.
- Uses simple physical threats that are immediately understandable.
- Refuses to discuss secrets rather than disguising the refusal.
- Respects demonstrated strength even while resenting the person who displayed it.

### Avoid

- Subtle political speeches.
- Making him another unintelligent Mudking.
- Treating him as a Library zealot.
- Repeating the original containment explanation after the Machine is gone.

## Character Contrast Guide

| Character | Relationship to uncertainty | Relationship to authority | Dialogue rhythm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Player Goblin | Acts before resolving it | Rejects it instinctively | Short and reactive |
| The Whim | Celebrates it | Evades responsibility | Calm and suggestive |
| The Archivist | Eliminates it | Embodies institutional authority | Measured and formal |
| Tooth Collector | Profits from it | Operates beside institutions | Quiet and transactional |
| Mudking | Does not recognize it | Invents personal grandeur | Pompous and confused |
| Chief Engineer | Studies it, then panics | Hides failure from authority | Fast and technical |
| Bog Tyrant | Hits it until it stays contained | Uses authority as force | Loud and direct |

## Knowledge Boundaries

- The Whim knows the released spark became the player but need not explain the complete chain immediately.
- The Chief Engineer eventually recognizes the relationship between the experiment and the player, but should reveal it over multiple Mountain visits.
- The Bog Tyrant knows the kobolds caused an impossible containment incident and that the Machine was transferred afterward. He does not understand the Machine's technical operation.
- The Archivist knows the Machine was assigned to the kobolds and later contained by the frogmen. Whether The Archivist knows that the kobold experiment released the player's spark remains deliberately unresolved.
- The current cast knows that someone possessed or found the Machine before the kobolds, but the identity of that group is reserved for a later biome.

## Maintenance Notes

- Update this document when a speaking character's worldview or voice changes.
- Keep player-facing titles synchronized with enemy, biome, encounter, dialogue, and Codex catalogs.
- Reveal secrets through staged dialogue inventory rather than compressing the full history into a first encounter.
- New enemy families should identify the goblin impulse preserved beneath their ordered form before their boss voice is authored.
