## Purpose

- Define the canonical high-level world framing for Dice Goblins.
- Give product, narrative, UX, and content work one shared source for the goblin point of view.
- Distinguish in-world truth from goblin belief where that distinction matters.

## Current World Framing

Dice Goblins is set in a world that appears bright, ordered, and prosperous on the surface.

That order is upheld by The Archivist and the Grand Library, an institution devoted to naming, cataloging, sorting, and stabilizing the world. For most of the world's inhabitants, that order is good. It makes life safer, clearer, and more predictable.

The player does not experience this world as a neutral observer. The player is a goblin warchief: an insider to goblin culture, a commander of a growing warband, and an active enemy of imposed order.

## The Goblin Point Of View

From the goblin perspective:

- chaos is the natural state of existence
- order is artificial and forced
- stability is a cage disguised as safety
- cracks, accidents, and improvisation are signs that the world is still alive

This does not mean the world agrees with the goblins. Most creatures see goblins as dangerous little monsters who ruin, steal, sabotage, and corrupt what civilized people have built.

That tension is core to the setting:

- the world sees order as protection
- goblins see order as suffocation
- the player acts from inside the goblin worldview rather than judging it from outside

## Goblins

Goblins are physical manifestations of The Whim, the closest thing the setting has to a god of chaos.

They are not beasts and should not be written as animal-minded creatures, but their visual identity should draw lightly from multiple animal influences:

- the smooth and warty skin qualities of toads
- the noses and ears of bats
- the teeth of piranha
- the mischievous attitude of a weasel

They are mostly humanoid, with only slightly exaggerated features. Their vibe should balance three truths at once:

- they are the protagonists of the game
- they are funny, disposable, scavenging tricksters
- they are secretly and sometimes overtly dangerous

Useful personality words:

- mischievous
- chaotic
- scavenging
- patchwork
- vicious
- ambitious
- disposable

## Tone For Goblin Writing

Goblin dialogue, flavor text, and item language should follow these rules:

- keep a fantasy-adventure or JRPG baseline rather than modern parody slang
- allow humor, swagger, and petty goblin ego
- allow adult-appropriate innuendo when it fits the voice
- avoid making goblins sound fully feral or fully innocent
- preserve the sense that even a joke may hide a threat

The closest tonal target is a protagonist-trickster energy similar to Stitch in animated `Lilo & Stitch`: endearing, impulsive, destructive, and more dangerous than the surface presentation suggests.

## The Archivist And The Grand Library

The Archivist is the champion of light who brought the world into order.

The Grand Library is the institutional expression of that victory. It exists to:

- classify the world
- preserve knowledge
- reduce disorder
- maintain continuity and control

From the world's perspective, this is largely a success. The Archivist is not framed here as a cartoon villain. The setting works better when order is genuinely beneficial for many people, even while goblins experience it as domination.

## Dice

Dice are not merely game abstractions. In-world, they are physical power sources that contain chaos magic.

They are shards of larger, ancient sources of chaos energy. Goblins gather dice because each die is a surviving fragment of older, greater powers that once gave chaos more presence in the world.

This framing should support both current gameplay and future content:

- dice can remain collectible mechanical loadout objects
- dice can carry lore weight as condensed chaos artifacts
- rarity, affixes, and accumulation can be described as the player gathering stronger pieces of suppressed chaos

## Goblin Objective

The goblins seek to accumulate enough dice to recreate larger chaos power sources.

Their ultimate goal is to restore the world to chaos by weakening the structures of order and empowering The Whim's influence once more.

In plain terms:

- the player is not defending civilization
- the player is raising a goblin force
- the player is gathering chaos power
- the player is trying to overturn the ordered world
