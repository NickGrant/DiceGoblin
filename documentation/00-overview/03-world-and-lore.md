# Dice Goblins - World and Lore

## Purpose

- Define the canonical high-level world framing for Dice Goblins.
- Give product, narrative, UX, and content work one shared source for the goblin point of view.
- Distinguish in-world truth from goblin belief where that distinction matters.
- Establish the relationship among goblins, The Whim, The Archivist, the Grand Library, the Wrong Machine, and goblin lineages.

## Current World Framing

Dice Goblins is set in a world that appears bright, ordered, and prosperous on the surface.

That order is upheld by The Archivist and the Grand Library, an institution devoted to naming, cataloging, sorting, and stabilizing the world. For most of the world's inhabitants, that order is good. It makes life safer, clearer, and more predictable.

The player does not experience this world as a neutral observer. The player is a goblin warchief: an insider to goblin culture, a commander of a growing warband, and an active enemy of imposed order.

## Chaos And Order

Chaos and order are not shorthand for good and evil.

In this setting:

- chaos permits possibility, improvisation, mutation, accidents, and unstable creation
- order permits continuity, safety, shared rules, classification, and predictable civilization
- too much chaos makes lasting structures difficult to preserve
- too much order suppresses change and removes anything that cannot be made stable

Goblins are not merely culturally chaotic. They are chaos incarnate: physical manifestations of The Whim and living sources of instability.

The Grand Library is fundamentally opposed to goblins because perfect order cannot fully contain or classify them. The conflict is existential even when neither side understands itself as evil.

## The Goblin Point Of View

From the goblin perspective:

- chaos is the natural state of existence
- order is artificial and forced
- stability is a cage disguised as safety
- cracks, accidents, and improvisation are signs that the world is still alive

This does not mean the world agrees with the goblins. Most creatures see goblins as dangerous little monsters who ruin, steal, sabotage, and corrupt what civilized people have built.

That tension is core to the setting:

- the world sees order as protection
- goblins see order as suffocation
- the player acts from inside the goblin worldview rather than judging it from outside
- most peoples have practical reasons to prefer a world without goblins

## Goblins

Goblins are physical manifestations of The Whim, the closest thing the setting has to a god of chaos.

They are not beasts and should not be written as animal-minded creatures, but their visual identity should draw lightly from multiple animal influences:

- the smooth and warty skin qualities of toads
- the noses and ears of bats
- the teeth of piranha
- the mischievous attitude of a weasel

They are mostly humanoid, with only slightly exaggerated features. Their vibe should balance three truths at once:

- they are the protagonists of the game
- they are funny, disposable, scavenging tricksters
- they are secretly and sometimes overtly dangerous

Useful personality words:

- mischievous
- chaotic
- scavenging
- patchwork
- vicious
- ambitious
- disposable

## Goblin Manifestation And Recruitment

Goblins do not need to rely only on an ordinary biological population to return.

The Whim can manifest goblin fragments when cracks form in the ordered world. The player's victories, recovered dice, released chaos, and disruption of Library-controlled regions weaken the conditions that kept goblins from existing.

The default story explanation for newly acquired goblins is:

- dormant fragments of The Whim are present throughout the world
- successful goblin action creates enough instability for those fragments to take form
- newly manifested goblins are drawn toward the growing warchief and warband
- a reward that grants a goblin may represent manifestation rather than a literal prisoner or recruit waiting at the encounter

This does not prohibit later stories about hidden survivors, isolated goblin communities, or other means of goblin continuation. Newly manifested fragments are the baseline explanation that makes the current reward loop coherent.

## Primordial Goblins

The primordial goblin is a form closer to unshaped chaos than the stable basic goblin used through most of the campaign.

The player's first appearance when conjured by The Whim foreshadows this lineage. Primordial Goblins are intended as a late-game unlock associated with defeating The Archivist and breaking the Grand Library's suppression of goblinkind.

They should feel like a return to goblin origins rather than a simple borrowed animal costume.

## Tone For Goblin Writing

Goblin dialogue, flavor text, and item language should follow these rules:

- keep a fantasy-adventure or JRPG baseline rather than modern parody slang
- allow humor, swagger, and petty goblin ego
- allow adult-appropriate innuendo when it fits the voice
- avoid making goblins sound fully feral or fully innocent
- preserve the sense that even a joke may hide a threat

The closest tonal target is a protagonist-trickster energy similar to Stitch in animated `Lilo & Stitch`: endearing, impulsive, destructive, and more dangerous than the surface presentation suggests.

## The Archivist And The Grand Library

The Archivist is the champion of light who brought the world into order.

The Grand Library is the institutional expression of that victory. It exists to:

- classify the world
- preserve knowledge
- reduce disorder
- maintain continuity and control
- define and enforce stable categories of existence

The Library's records are not merely administrative. Its cataloging, laws, and magic help stabilize reality around the version of the world the institution recognizes.

The Archivist eradicated historical goblinkind through two connected methods:

- direct extermination by Library forces
- forced stabilization that imposed order on chaotic goblin forms and transformed some of them into stable, classifiable creature lineages

This is why the Archivist describes a surviving goblin as an error in the record. Correcting the record can include correcting reality until goblins no longer exist within it.

The Archivist's agents should resemble clerical templars:

- heavy armor and disciplined formations
- religious or bureaucratic authority
- formal magical training
- specialized spells that bind, suppress, classify, and neutralize chaos
- police-state enforcement presented as necessary protection

From the world's perspective, this system is largely a success. The Archivist is not a cartoon villain. The setting works better when order is genuinely beneficial for many people, even while goblins experience it as domination and annihilation.

## Other Peoples Under Order

Most organized peoples encountered by the player are under the rule or influence of the Grand Library.

Their histories may include:

- former alliances with goblins before the triumph of order
- descent from goblins or goblin lineages altered by forced stabilization
- independent origins followed by acceptance of Library rule
- old injuries caused by goblin chaos
- pragmatic dependence on the safety, roads, trade, records, and institutions created by order

Most do not consider the return of goblins good news. They may dislike the Library's control while still believing that they are better off not being goblins and not having goblins in the world.

Defeating a local ruler does not automatically liberate a grateful population. In many regions, the player is deliberately destroying a system that local inhabitants consider preferable to chaos.

## Dice

Dice are not merely game abstractions. In-world, they are physical power sources that contain chaos magic.

They are shards of larger, ancient sources of chaos energy. Goblins gather dice because each die is a surviving fragment of older, greater powers that once gave chaos more presence in the world.

This framing should support both current gameplay and future content:

- dice can remain collectible mechanical loadout objects
- dice can carry lore weight as condensed chaos artifacts
- rarity, affixes, and accumulation can be described as the player gathering stronger pieces of suppressed chaos
- salvaging dice can release Raw Chaos from their more stable physical form

## Raw Chaos

Raw Chaos is chaos power released from a constructed or stabilized object, currently obtained by salvaging dice.

It is distinct from teeth and should not behave as merely a rarer form of ordinary currency.

Raw Chaos is used for systems that construct, reconstruct, or manipulate chaotic forms, including the Wrong Machine and goblin lineage restoration.

## The Wrong Machine

The Wrong Machine is a proper name, not a diagnosis of a broken or incorrectly functioning device.

It is an ancient goblin or Whim-aligned contraption built to construct things from chaos. Historical goblins used it to shape chaotic material into useful forms, including goblin lineages.

The machine was lost when the Grand Library defeated goblinkind and eventually passed into enemy control. During the current campaign:

- kobold tinkerers provide information about its location
- the machine is held by the frogmen's boss in the Swamps
- reclaiming it completes the opening story arc
- restoring it gives the player a way to combine Raw Chaos with lineage materials recovered from enemy families

The Wrong Machine becomes the central axis for unlocking goblin subspecies and restoring forms that the Library's order erased or stabilized away.

Its exact recipes, costs, and mechanical restrictions belong in system documentation rather than this lore baseline.

## Goblin Lineages And Splicing

A goblin lineage is a reconstructed goblin form influenced by traits recovered from a stable creature family.

Examples currently established or planned include:

- Pig Goblins from pig enemies
- Lizard Goblins from kobolds
- Frog Goblins from frogmen
- Bug Goblins from insect enemies
- Shadow Goblins from shadow or wraith enemies
- Stone Goblins from stone creatures or constructs
- Bat Goblins from aerial creature families
- Primordial Goblins as the late-game return to original chaos

Use **goblin-kin** or **kin** for the approved game terminology. The lore framing is reconstruction through chaos rather than modern laboratory genetics.

The intended process is:

- defeat a creature family and recover associated lineage materials
- release or gather Raw Chaos
- use the Wrong Machine to reconstruct a goblin lineage from those ingredients

Lineages may provide combat traits, exploration abilities, or both. Some should act as traversal keys that reveal paths and discoveries in earlier regions.

## Teeth

Teeth are ordinary monster currency.

They are physical, portable, recognizable, and appropriately brutal for goblin commerce. They do not need a hidden magical or cosmological purpose.

The Tooth Collector participates in this economy as a vendor and recurring character, not because teeth secretly power the central conflict.

## Goblin Objective

The goblins seek to accumulate enough dice and Raw Chaos to recreate larger chaos power sources, restore lost goblin lineages, and rebuild goblinkind.

Their ultimate goal is to weaken the structures of order, defeat The Archivist, and allow The Whim's influence and chaotic possibility to return to the world.

In plain terms:

- the player is not defending civilization
- the player is raising a goblin force
- the player is gathering chaos power
- the player is reconstructing goblin forms erased by order
- the player is trying to overturn the ordered world

Defeating The Archivist resolves the main campaign but does not end play. The world remains available for repeatable runs, bounties, hidden discoveries, remaining lineage unlocks, and the consequences of weakened central order.

## Usage Notes

- Use this document as the canonical lore baseline for new overview, UX, dialogue, and flavor-writing work.
- Use `documentation/00-overview/04-story-and-biome-progression.md` for the planning-level chapter sequence and biome beats.
- If a future narrative doc introduces competing interpretations, explicitly distinguish between objective setting fact and goblin belief.
- Keep mechanical docs grounded in implementation, but allow them to reference this file when explaining what dice, lineages, Raw Chaos, or goblins mean in-world.
- Do not reinterpret the Wrong Machine as an enemy machine that was made wrong or corrupted unless this document is deliberately revised.
