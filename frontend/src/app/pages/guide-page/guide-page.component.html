Overview

What you are actually managing

Dice Goblins loops between two modes. In HQ, you assemble squads, upgrade the roster, buy dice, and plan promotions. On a run, you commit a squad to a chain of encounters where health attrition, pathing, and die quality all matter.

Permanent progress mostly comes from unlocks, better dice, and broader unit options. Temporary progress happens inside a run, where the goal is to survive enough combat to cash out rewards and return stronger than you left.

Current Rules

What progression looks like right now

Units can promote starting at level 6, but every class can now stay through level 10 to claim one of two capstones first. The Academy unlocks the roster tree, while the shop and your run rewards handle the economy side of long-term growth.

If you are signed in, acquired feature unlocks and base unit unlocks are marked below so the guide reads like a live codex instead of a static document.

Promotion

How units grow and branch

Promotion always advances a unit by one tier. Eligibility starts at level 6, while level 10 offers a capstone choice for the current class. Promoting early speeds up access to new branches, while staying longer locks in class mastery first.

Promotion still consumes two matching supporting units. The primary unit keeps its identity and carries forward into the chosen destination, resetting to level 1 for the new tier.

Branching

Current promotion paths

Tier 1 lines currently branch into Enforcer or Pit Fighter, Bulwark or Shieldbreaker, Deadeye or Trapper, Warcaller or Mascot, and Trickshot or Plaguehand. Tier 3 promotions still expect the authored rare region item.

If a level 10 unit has not chosen its capstone yet, the promotion flow requires that decision before the tier change can be confirmed.

Dice Rules

How dice connect to abilities

Dice are equipped into ability slots, not into a shared unit-wide inventory. If a slot is empty, the roll resolves as `1`, which keeps the loadout functional but much weaker than a fully built kit.

Reordering abilities changes when those rolls matter because actions fire in loadout order through the combat scheduler, not all at once.

Affixes

What makes one die different from another

Size defines the raw roll ceiling. Rarity changes the material tier, value, and overall affix budget. Affixes are what turn a die from generic throughput into a piece aimed at offense, defense, sustain, or burst.

Combat

How battles actually resolve

Combat takes place on mirrored 3x3 formations. Front and back rows matter, so tougher units usually absorb the first pressure while ranged or support units benefit from safer lanes behind them.

Abilities execute in order using the combat scheduler. Status effects such as Poison, Bolstered, Sleep, and Bleeding resolve in their own phases, and dead units stop taking later actions in the same tick.

Run Resolution

What happens when a run ends

During a run, HP attrition persists across encounters until you find a rest node. If you fail or abandon the expedition, the run ends immediately and defeated units lose current-level XP progress, but they do not lose levels.

After the run summary cleanup, units heal fully, statuses clear, and rechargeable abilities recover. Success mainly changes the reward payout, not the cleanup pattern itself.

Run Flow

Expedition checklist

Before the run

Choose a squad, inspect unit health, and make sure the right dice are attached to your highest-impact abilities.

During the run

Balance greed against survival. Loot grows the warband, but rest nodes protect a promising expedition from collapsing.

After the run

Use rewards to buy permanent power, unlock fresh unit lines, and prepare the next run with a stronger roster.
